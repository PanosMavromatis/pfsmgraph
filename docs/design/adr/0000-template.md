## Context

What situation forced a decision. The constraints, the pressures, the thing that would
have gone wrong without deciding. Write it so a reader who has never seen the PRD can
follow.

## Decision

What was decided, stated in the active voice and in the present tense: "The encoder
raises on unseen symbols by default." One paragraph if possible.

## Consequences

### Positive

What this buys, including consequences that only show up later.

### Negative / costs

What it costs, what it forecloses, and what ongoing discipline it demands. This section
is not optional padding — an ADR with no costs listed is usually an ADR that has not
been thought through.

## Alternatives considered

Each rejected option, and *why* it was rejected. This is the most perishable knowledge
in the document: within a year, nobody remembers what was ruled out, and the rejected
option gets proposed again.

## Evidence

Empirical findings, measurements, experiments, and external facts the decision rests on.
Distinguish what was *observed* from what was *reasoned*. Omit the section when the
decision rests on reasoning alone.

## Open

Anything deliberately left unresolved, and what will resolve it. An ADR may be `Proposed`
precisely because this section is non-empty.

- [x] Implement the torch forward pass and autograd E-step
  > **Q:** Which tensors receive the gradients: the probabilities (counts `p * p.grad`) or log-parameters (counts `θ.grad`)?
  > **A:** The probabilities. `p · ∂log P/∂p` is `∂log P/∂log p` by the chain rule, so the identity is the plan's, and the forward pass starts from numpy's exact inputs rather than `exp(log p)`, which is not bit-exactly `p`; dead arcs need no `log(0)` leaf.
  > **Q:** Which dtype and device does `backend="torch"` use?
  > **A:** float64 on CPU only. ADR 0021 lets no environment choose where a call runs, and device placement is the batching subgoal's (ADR 0021 Open); float32 stays ADR 0020's Open item.
  > **Q:** What floor do the `[torch]` extra and the dev-group entry declare?
  > **A:** `torch>=2.13`, the version the backend is measured on, as `cpu-parallel`'s floor is numba's measured version.
  > **Q:** Which stopping-rule keywords does `baum_welch` make public?
  > **A:** All four, `batch_cycles`, `change_bits`, `patience`, `max_cycles`, defaulting to `run-converge`'s constants.
  > **Q:** Does the private loop become public by renaming or by a wrapper?
  > **A:** Renamed in place: `_em` → `baum_welch`, `_EMResult` → `BaumWelchResult`, both exported, and the EM tests switch to the public names.

Goal 3's test must assert the fixture's description length is finite.
  - [x] E-step counts from `.grad` on the log-parameters
    > **Note:** as `p * p.grad` on probability leaves (goal 2's first Q&A), which is the log-parameter gradient by the chain rule. No `nan` anywhere, and the zero pattern of all three arrays matches numpy's exactly, dead arcs and reserved fibres included. The largest absolute difference is `3.3e-15` (init), `4.0e-13` (transition), `2.3e-13` (emission) at N ≤ 400, and `1.2e-12` on the fixtures, whose counts reach 1268. **Relative error is the wrong measure**: on counts near underflow it reached `5e57` for init counts, and `4e-7` even above a `1e-12` floor, while the absolute difference stayed at rounding. Scaled by `N·eps` the worst was 0.5 (init), 28.7 (transition, S = 2, N = 2000) and 6.3 (emission), so the error grows with count size as well as `N`, and goal 3 should set an absolute or mixed tolerance, not a relative one.
    > **Note:** *(Added by goal 3.)* Those measurements checked the zero pattern and magnitudes but never the sign, and the kernel as committed in `58942fe` could return **negative counts**, down to `-3.0e-19`, which crash `baum_welch(..., backend="torch")` in `HMMParams`. Random models could not show it, since their Dirichlet emissions are never near zero; four of the first six EM trajectories reached it within 17-63 cycles, once parameters had fallen to `1e-48`-`1e-133`. The cause was differentiating `column / q` through `q`, a cancellation of near-equal terms; goal 3 detaches the scale factors instead. A **second** defect followed on longer runs: `transition_p · ∂T` and `output_p · ∂O` were computed separately, so on an arc of probability `8e-322` they rounded a whole subnormal unit apart and the re-estimated fibre summed to `1.002`. Goal 3 takes the gradient on the arc table `w` instead, with transition counts as `Σ_u` of its per-symbol counts, as the reference forms them.

- [x] Assert the count identity as an ADR 0003 cross-backend test
  > **Q:** How is the torch kernel kept from returning negative counts?
  > **A:** By detaching the scale factors and backpropagating from `ln Σ_j column_N` alone, which is still `∂ln P` since a constant rescaling leaves a log-derivative unchanged. The graph then holds only `+`, `*` and division by constants, so every count is a sum of nonnegative terms. Measured on the failing trajectories: 0 negatives where the committed kernel had 1-3 per state, and the same accuracy on 240 random cases. Clamping was declined because it hides the cancellation rather than removing it.
  > **Q:** What shape does the cross-backend test take?
  > **A:** Two layers: the count identity at the kernel through `_resolve("baum_welch", backend)`, in a labelled non-public section as `test_viterbi.py` has, since the public call cannot see counts; and the public `baum_welch` on each backend through its own `backend` fixture, against `backend="python"`, with EM trajectories that reach near-zero parameters among the cases.
  > **Q:** The public runs then crashed torch on a fibre summing to `1.002` over an arc of probability `8e-322`: separately computed transition and emission gradients round a subnormal unit apart. Take the gradient on the arc table `w` and form transition counts as `Σ_u` of its per-symbol counts?
  > **A:** Yes. `Σ_k emission == transition` then holds by construction, as it does in the reference, which adds the same ξ into both. Measured: 0 of 117 constructed subnormal-arc models failed re-estimation, against 59 with separate gradients; 30 of 30 public runs matched the reference.
  > **Q:** What bound do the kernel-level count and description-length assertions use?
  > **A:** `4 · N · eps · max(1, x)` per element, `x` being the reference's count or bits.
  > **Q:** What bound does the public `baum_welch` agreement test use?
  > **A:** `cycles`, `converged` and `degenerate_states` exactly, and fitted parameters within `abs=1e-12`.
  - [x] numpy ξ/γ counts vs torch gradients over generated and constructed models
    > **Note:** `tests/test_baum_welch_backends.py`, 122 tests over both backends, about 30 s: a public section (8 generated trainings and `m008` through `baum_welch`, plus an impossible record) and a kernel section through `_resolve` (28 size/length combinations with exact structural zeros, the three fixtures after asserting their bits are finite, 6 EM trajectories of 30 cycles, 12 constructed subnormal arcs, empty and impossible records), every state re-estimated through `_re_estimate`. **Each defect fails a named test**, checked by running the file against each kernel: the `58942fe` kernel fails 11 (two public runs, a count bound, eight subnormal arcs), the scale-detached one 8 (the subnormal arcs), the arc-leaf kernel none. Neither defect was reachable by random models alone: 240 random trainings over 200 cycles never hit the subnormal case, which a single arc of `20-400` subnormal units hits in half of all models.
  - [x] Measure and record the tolerance actually needed
    > **Note:** measured over 240 random E-steps, 15 000 EM-trajectory states and the three fixtures: every count within `N·eps·max(1, count)` (worst 1.00; fixtures 0.03), every description length within `N·eps·max(1, bits)` (worst 0.21, absolute worst `2.8e-14` bits), both held to 4× in the tests. A whole `baum_welch` run matched in `cycles`, `converged` and `degenerate_states` in 33 of 33 runs, with parameters within `3.4e-14` (fixtures `8.2e-15`), held to `1e-12`. **ADR 0020's "a few ulps" is the wrong form, not the wrong size**: relative to the bits it reaches 15 578 eps on records costing a fraction of a bit late in EM, and relative to the counts `5e57` near underflow. Underflow also decides zero patterns: 4 of 15 000 states had an emission count of `1e-323` where the reference had `0`, so only structural zeros are asserted exactly. On torch 2.13 CPU a fixture run takes ~3.8 s against ~1.4 s for the reference.
  > **Done:** the count identity is an ADR 0003 cross-backend suite in two layers, it found and now pins two torch-kernel defects that crashed `baum_welch`, and the kernel takes its gradients on the arc table with detached scale factors. The tolerance is recorded above in the shape the error actually has.
